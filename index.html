📊 Periodic Trends

How properties change across the Periodic Table

Across a Period (Li → Ne)
Li Be B C N O F Ne
→ Decreases across period
Down a Group (Li → Cs)
Li Na K Rb Cs
↓ Increases down group
Atomic Radius
Atoms get smaller across a period (more protons pull electrons closer). Atoms get larger down a group (more electron shells).

📜 Periodic Table & History

How the Periodic Table was discovered

1829

Johann Döbereiner

Law of Triads — he noticed that elements could be grouped in threes with similar properties. The middle element had an atomic weight close to the average of the other two.
Why it matters
Each scientist added a key idea that led to the modern Periodic Table we use today.

🔋 Galvanic Cell

Chemical energy → Electrical energy

Zinc – Copper (Daniell Cell)
Anode (−): Zn → Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻ (oxidation)
Cathode (+): Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu (reduction)
Electrons flow from zinc to copper through the wire.

How it works
• Electrons leave the anode and travel through the wire to the cathode.
• The salt bridge lets ions move so charge balance is maintained.
• Chemical energy is converted into electrical energy.

⚡ Electrolysis

Electricity forces non-spontaneous reactions to occur

Cathode (−)
Reduction happens here. Cations gain electrons.
Anode (+)
Oxidation happens here. Anions lose electrons.
Electrolyte
Molten or dissolved compound that conducts and is decomposed.
Molten Sodium Chloride
Cathode (−): Na⁺ + e⁻ → Na (sodium metal)
Anode (+): 2Cl⁻ → Cl₂ + 2e⁻ (chlorine gas)
Overall: 2NaCl(l) → 2Na(l) + Cl₂(g)

Important Rules
• At the cathode: the ion that is most easily reduced is discharged.
• At the anode: the ion that is most easily oxidised is discharged.
• In aqueous solutions, H₂O can compete with the ions of the electrolyte.
• Concentrated solutions often favour discharge of the anion (e.g. Cl⁻ → Cl₂).
